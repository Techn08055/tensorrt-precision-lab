# Conv2D

Second CUDA-kernel module: take real convolutions out of `resnet/` and run them as
GEMMs, starting from the heaviest layers in the per-layer profiles in `../profiling/`.
Builds on [`../gemm/`](../gemm/); see the repo root [`README.md`](../README.md).

So the per-layer-profiler figures used above (0.038-0.043 ms) overstate TensorRT's time by ~2x, and the earlier "level with / just under TensorRT" readings were wrong. TensorRT's chosen kernels are `sm80_xmma_fprop_implicit_gemm_*_nhwckrsc_nhwc` (implicit GEMM in NHWC, multi-stage pipelined, 64x32 to 64x128 tiles, no split-K) with `f16f16_f16f16_f16` in the name, i.e. FP16 accumulation; its error against ONNX is 0.037-0.060, ~10x ours. Tested one explanation: cuBLAS on our im2col buffer with `CUBLAS_COMPUTE_16F` is **not** faster than with FP32 accumulation (0.0413 vs 0.0414 ms) and its error rises 0.0057 → 0.013, so FP16 accumulation alone doesn't account for it in cuBLAS. Not yet isolated: NHWC layout, pipeline depth, or FP16 accumulation inside our own kernel
- [x] What TensorRT's ~2x came from (`Conv_850`, all numbers same-run, three runs within 1%, then checked on `Conv_856`/`Conv_842`):
  1. **Hardware:** FP16 accumulation runs the tensor cores at exactly 2.00x the FP32-accumulate rate on this GPU at every block count — [`../gemm/profiling/mma_rate.cu`](../gemm/profiling/mma_rate.cu), [`../gemm/profiling/mma_rate.txt`](../gemm/profiling/mma_rate.txt). Peak is ~2.8 TFLOPS per SM with FP32 accumulation (~55.7 for all 20 SMs; ~42 when the GPU is in its slower clock state, the same 1.33x as our 0.041 vs 0.031 ms runs). Our 0.031 ms kernel ran at ~7.4 TFLOPS, ~13% of that, so it wasn't limited by tensor-core math
  2. **FP16 accumulators in our kernel** (`gemm_wmma_splitk<0, half>`): no gain at the best 12 splits (0.0314 vs 0.0313 ms), 7% at 1 split, error 4.5x higher (0.0021 → 0.0095 after ReLU). Not the cause on its own
  3. **`cp.async` pipeline** (`gemm_wmma_splitk_pipe<STAGES>`, the next tiles copy into shared memory while the current one is computed): 0.152 → 0.080 ms at 1 split (8 blocks), 0.0314 → 0.0278 ms at 12 splits. Two stages are as good as or better than three or four
  4. **Padded shared-memory rows** (`PAD = 8` halves, so the 16 rows a fragment load reads don't start in the same banks): **0.0278 → 0.0215 ms** at 12 splits, 0.080 → 0.060 ms at 8 blocks. Biggest single step. With padding, FP16 accumulators help at 8 blocks (0.060 → 0.046-0.048 ms) but only 2% at 12 splits (0.0211 ms), so FP32 accumulation stays the default
  5. **Result:** all three 3x3 layers at **0.0215-0.0218 ms with bias + ReLU** (2 stages, pad 8, 12 splits, FP32 accumulators, error vs ReLU(ONNX) 0.0021-0.0031), **1.46x faster than im2col + cuBLAS** and ~1.45x behind TensorRT's 0.015 ms (was 2.1x). Nsight Systems split of the 0.0215 ms path (trace taken in the slower clock state, so shares only): GEMM 76%, im2col 9%, reduce 10%, launch gaps 5%. TensorRT does the same work in one conv kernel plus two layout copies, so roughly half the remaining gap is our extra im2col/reduce kernels and half the GEMM kernel itself — estimated from the shares, not measured kernel-for-kernel, because the TensorRT trace is dominated by its tactic-timing runs
